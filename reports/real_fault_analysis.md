# Real fault replay

Source: `data/phytosense_trial_log.csv` (329,271 source rows; 315,899 unique timestamps after deterministic duplicate coalescing). The wall clock is naive and has no timezone marker. Firmware logs an average of 20 ADC samples per cycle; for this file per-cycle min/max/n_sat are absent, so high-rail detection is approximated by logged average >=4090.

Known rail-valued source rows (>=4090 or <=50): **93,154** (high=93,154, low=0); 93,049 unique rail timestamps remain after coalescing. Grouping rail rows separated by at most 10 seconds produces 109 episodes; this is an operational grouping, with actual timestamps and elapsed durations in `out/revised/real_fault_episodes.csv`. These rows are spread across the recording, including early isolated samples, so the aggregate must not be described as one continuous 93,154-second episode.

Firmware uses 20-read average, digital leads-off, average/sample saturation rails, rolling 60-sample standard deviation <2, and absolute one-step jump >1200. Priority is contact, high rail, low rail, flat, jump. A good sample is scored against the current baseline and then pushed; baseline is a 1800-value rolling **mean/sample SD**, begins alerting after 200 values, threshold |z|>3, persistence 10. On 600 consecutive rejected cycles it clears the ring; this retains no baseline state. Flat ring, previous-sample state, and deviation run are not cleared by that reset. The replay mirrors sample-count behavior; missing elapsed time does not consume state. This cannot reconstruct the original early firmware processing or hidden ADC sample saturation.

The replay starts with an empty baseline at the beginning of this CSV, which is primarily a faulty early stream; it is not a shared known-clean initialized state. Its ungated arm admits all rail-valued samples; gated arm excludes reasons derived from available fields. This shows algorithmic admission, not a calibrated causal estimate of a deployed firmware run. Results and time series are in the CSV outputs. The reference uses the first up to 3600 nonrail, gate-accepted samples in chronological order; this period is not guaranteed biologically stable.

No claim of real biological recovery is made: the log has extensive gaps and separated recording sessions; a transition from fault to later clean data cannot be treated as continuous recovery. See benchmark timestamp handling and the source data audit for gap limits.
